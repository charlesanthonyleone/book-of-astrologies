Babylonian astrology began as the heavens read as a tablet. The gods "wrote" in the sky, and scribes (ṭupšarru) read the signs as omens for the king and the land. Only in the last five centuries BCE did those omens turn into personal horoscopes. This chapter reads your birth the way a scribe of Uruk or Babylon could have: by planet, star, Moon, number, and omen. It finishes with the prophetic texts about "a king who will arise".

1a. The sign-by-sign walk (this tradition's equivalent of houses) Interpretation

Babylonian astrology had no houses. Its equivalents were the twelve zodiacal constellations (MUL.APIN-based, standardised c. 400 BCE) and the horizon events: rising (napāḫu), culminating (ziqpu), setting (rabû). Here is the walk through all twelve:

  1. HUN — Hired Man (Aries): Saturn at 6°. Labour, wages and the hired worker. Saturn here makes the steady, paid toil a lifelong theme.
  2. MUL.MUL — Stars/Pleiades (Taurus): empty. It is Venus's dodecatemorion and the Moon's niṣirti (Taurus 3), so it holds a hidden reserve of emotional exaltation.
  3. MAŠ.TAB.BA — Great Twins (Gemini): empty. Saturn's dodecatemorion falls here, so siblings and pairs are shaped by duty.
  4. ALLA — Crab (Cancer): Moon at 12°. Jupiter's secret house (Cancer 15) lies here too, so the home and the inner self join the strongest planet's place of exaltation.
  5. A — Lion (Leo): Mars at 9°, just past LUGAL, the "King" star. The warrior stands beside the king's star but is faint and distant.
  6. ABSIN — Furrow (Virgo): Sun 25° and Venus 21°, with Spica (the Furrow's ear of grain) nearby. This is the place of harvest, grain and Šala, and the day-light of your life shines here.
  7. RÍN — Scales (Libra): Mercury at 8°. Šamaš's Scales, the scales of judgement, hold Nabû, the scribe-god.
  8. GÍR.TAB — Scorpion (Scorpio): empty. It is the dodecatemorion of both Mars and Jupiter, so a concealed fighting edge lies in reserve.
  9. PA.BIL.SAG — Pabilsag (Sagittarius): the Midheaven at 0°. The archer-god stands at the top of the sky, so a public life of aim and pursuit.
  10. SUḪUR.MAŠ — Goat-Fish (Capricorn): empty. It is Ea's creature, Mercury's dodecatemorion and Mars's niṣirti (Capricorn 28), so deep-water wisdom serves the mind.
  11. GU — Great One (Aquarius): Jupiter at 25° and the Ascendant at 26°. The great water-pouring god rises with Marduk, which sets the identity of your life.
  12. zib.ME — Tails (Pisces): empty. Venus's niṣirti (Pisces 27) lies here, a hidden harbour for love that the birth itself does not show.

What this means for you. Read as a scribe, your sky reads like this: a king-planet rising in the sign of the Great One, a public aim at the top of the sky, the daylight working in the fields of harvest and judgement, and the home kept in the Crab under Jupiter's secret blessing.

What this means for you. The nativity tablet's best line, Jupiter coming forth, fits your birth exactly. By the scribes' own rule you were born into a 'normal' and 'rich' life: order, sufficiency and long-term increase. Nothing from the Mars or Venus warning clauses applies.

16. Integrated Reading Interpretation

A scribe of Uruk handed your tablet would read it like this: "When Marduk came forth on the eastern horizon in the Great One, in Tašrītu, day 22, Šamaš standing in the west: the child will be normal; he will be rich; the gods will be favourable to him." Your character is organised around Jupiter: generosity, judgement, protective authority and a love of order. The Sun (Šamaš, justice) and Mercury (Nabû, the scribe) supply a judge's and record-keeper's mind. Saturn adds endurance through honest work. Mars is a tamed warrior who has passed the King star. Venus is the one quieter area: love and pleasure arrive late and then return strongly. In prophecy you most clearly echo the just, Anu-honouring king of the Uruk Prophecy and the Marduk Prophecy's restorer.
