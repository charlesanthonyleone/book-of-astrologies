Charles Leone — born 13 October 1998, 17:25 EDT (21:25 UT), Johnstown, Pennsylvania (40.33°N, 78.92°W). Lives in Fayetteville, North Carolina (35.05°N, 78.88°W).

A word on respect and honesty

The traditions in this chapter belong to living peoples. Most of them are sky calendars and navigation sciences, not horoscopes. For each item, this chapter says whether the tradition itself applies it to a person's birth or whether it is only a calendar or sky reading. Where it is only a calendar or sky reading, the "What this means for you" note is a reflective reading and is labelled as one. It is not a claim about what the tradition teaches.

None of these traditions has texts that describe the faces of people born under particular stars. So no portrait faces were drawn from tradition texts, because no such texts exist.

Some imagery was deliberately left out because the traditions restrict it:

  • Haudenosaunee False Face (Gagohsa) masks
  • Hopi katsina
  • The sacred pipe
  • Eagle-feather headdresses
  • Depictions of White Buffalo Calf Woman or the Peacemaker
  • Personal tā moko
  • Te Kooti's flags
  • Images of Lono's akua

Computations were done in Python with ephem; the values are stored in deep/28_calc.json. At the moment of birth the Sun was 12.7° above the horizon, so this was a daylight birth, 74 minutes before sunset at 18:39 EDT.

1. Hawaiian

1.1 The 30 moon nights (pō mahina)

What it is: Hawaiian months count from Hilo, the first crescent. In 1998 Hilo was 21 September and the full moon fell on Hoku (5 October). This birth fell in daylight after night 22, ʻOlekūlua, a last-quarter night. Kepelino's notes say farmers disliked ʻOlekūlua for planting but it was good for torch-fishing. The alternative count gives night 21, ʻOlekūkahi.

The Moon was 23.18 days old and 35% lit, and waning.

Is it applied to birth? It is a calendar of work, fishing and planting. Kepelino describes the character of each night, but not as a fixed fate for a child.

What this means for you (reflective): You arrived on a "not for planting, good for fishing by torchlight" night. The image is of someone who does best in the gathering phase rather than the sowing phase: finding what is already in the water, and working by a small, focused light.

1.2 The Hawaiian month and Makahiki

What it is: David Malo gives the order of the months. Makahiki opens when Makaliʻi (the Pleiades) rises at dusk. In 1998 that was about 15 November at Johnstown and about 19 November at Honolulu. Using Malo's order with that anchor, the birth lunation is Kamahoemua, about five weeks before Makahiki.

Is it applied to birth? No. It is a calendar reading.

What this means for you (reflective): You were born in the last working stretch before the season of peace and games. Your instinct may be to finish the harvest before you rest.

1.3 Kumulipo

What it is: A cosmogonic chant in 16 wā: the 1st to 7th are Pō (darkness) and the 8th to 16th are Ao (light). It traces genealogy from coral polyps to chiefs.

Is it applied to birth? It does not assign people to a wā. It is a genealogy and a cosmogony.

What this means for you (reflective): A daylight birth near sunset sits symbolically at the edge between Ao and Pō. This is an image, not a teaching.

  • Hōkūleʻa (Arcturus) at 42.0°, Lā Kona

  • Rēhua (Antares) at 21.4°, Haka Kona

Is it applied to birth? No. The compass is for navigation. This table is a sky record, not a reading.

What this means for you (reflective): Hōkūleʻa, the zenith star of Hawaiʻi and the namesake of the voyaging canoe, stood high in the west-southwest as you were born. The Sun and Venus were setting together in ʻĀina Kona. Your sky was one of homing and arrival rather than departure.

Section synthesis (Hawaiʻi): A late-moon, harvest-end, sunset birth under Hōkūleʻa. None of these markers is assigned at birth by Hawaiian tradition. Together they give a reflective image of someone who gathers and steers home.

2. Māori

2.1 Maramataka

What it is: Taking Whiro (the new moon) as 21 September, and using the Te Ara Ngāti Kahungunu list, night 22 is Korekore-hahani, "a fairly good day". The alternative count gives Tangaroa-ā-mua, which is good for fishing. Lists vary by iwi.

Is it applied to birth? It is a working calendar.

What this means for you (reflective): Your night is a moderate, steady one that leans toward Tangaroa. That echoes the Hawaiian fishing reading.

2.2 Matariki

What it is: In Rangi Mātāmua's work there are nine stars, each tied to food, water, weather or the dead. They mark the new year in midwinter.

Is it applied to birth? No. It is seasonal. At your birth the cluster was below the horizon.

What this means for you (reflective): You were born in the half of the year when Matariki is out of sight in the evening, which is the season of tending rather than celebrating.

2.3 Tohunga "birth-star" lore

What it is: Searches found no verified Māori system that reads a destiny from the star at birth. The tohi birth rite dedicates a child through atua and whakapapa (Te Ara), not through a star.

Is it applied to birth? Unverified, so it is not applied here.

What this means for you: Nothing is claimed. It is better to say that than to invent something.

Section synthesis (Aotearoa): Two calendar echoes, fishing and a moderate night. There is no natal star assignment.

3. Tonga, Samoa and Tahiti

What it is: The Pleiades are Mataliki in Tonga, Mataliʻi in Samoa and Matariʻi in Tahiti. Tahiti divides the year into Matariʻi i niʻa (abundance, from about November) and Matariʻi i raro (the lean season, from about May). Your birth falls in Matariʻi i raro.

Is it applied to birth? No. It is seasonal.

What this means for you (reflective): You were born late in the lean season, just before the turn to plenty. This is a pattern of patience that pays off.

Section synthesis: One shared star family and one seasonal marker.

4. Haudenosaunee and Seneca

What it is: The Mohawk name for October is Kenténha, "nature getting poor". The 13-moon cycle places the Harvest Moon around this time; Seneca moon names vary by longhouse. The Pleiades are the Seven Dancers, whose position at the zenith times Midwinter. The Big Dipper is the bear and its hunters, and the bear's blood colours the autumn leaves.

Is it applied to birth? It is a calendar and a set of sky stories.

What this means for you (reflective): You were born exactly in red-leaf season, the time of the Bear-hunt story. It is a harvest-and-thanks month.

5. Lenape, Lumbee and Cherokee

5.1 Lenape

What it is: Harrington records that the Unami annual ceremony was held between 10 and 20 October, when the leaves turned yellow. 13 October falls inside that window, which is a calendar match. The Munsee name for the October moon is Punihle-Waniipakw Niipaahum. Lenape skylore also links the Big Dipper to a bear.

Is it applied to birth? No. It is a calendar.

What this means for you (reflective): You were born in western Pennsylvania during the traditional Lenape thanksgiving window, a time of renewal.

5.2 Lumbee

No documented Lumbee star lore was found. This is a gap, and nothing has been invented to fill it.

What this means for you: You live in Fayetteville, close to Robeson County. The honest position is to learn directly from the Lumbee community rather than from books.

5.3 Cherokee

What it is: The October moon is the Harvest Moon (Duninudi / Nvda Tsiyahloha), the season of the Great New Moon ceremony.

Is it applied to birth? No. It is a calendar.

What this means for you (reflective): For both your birthplace and your home, October is the Southeastern harvest-and-new-year season.

Residence note: from Fayetteville, Canopus rises to about 2.3°, low in the south. From Johnstown it never rises at all.

Section synthesis (Eastern Woodlands): Three independent October harvest and renewal calendars all match your birth date. This is the strongest calendar alignment in the chapter.

6. Lakota (Goodman, Lakota Star Knowledge)

What it is: The Lakota October moon is Čhaŋwápekasná Wí. Goodman describes the sacred spring round, which follows the Sun from the equinox to the solstice through Aries and Triangulum, then the Pleiades, then Gemini. Your Sun in sidereal Virgo is not part of that round.

Is it applied to birth? No. It is a ceremonial calendar.

What this means for you (reflective): Your birth falls outside the ceremonial sky round, in the hunting and preparation season.

Prophecy markers

7. Prophecy marker test

Each prophecy is compared with your chart marker by marker. There are no scores and no verdicts. Markers you carry are listed first.

Pahana (Hopi) - Markers you carry: male (the true white brother); born in the East and living in the East (the prophecy's direction of return). - Markers read symbolically: the red cap or cloak as Mars, the red planet, in Leo; the missing corner of the tablet as the piece a father's line restores, Platt, the "flat" stone-plate in English.

Seventh Fire, "New People" (Anishinaabe, Benton-Banai) - Markers you carry: you belong to a generation of seekers who look back to the old ways. This marker is collective. - Note: the Eighth Fire is a collective choice between two roads, so no marker is personal by design.

White Buffalo Calf Woman's return (Lakota) - Markers you carry, through the mother's line: the holy woman who brings the pipe is carried in your mother's name, Roseann Mary, the Western holy mother; you were born in the Moon of Falling Leaves, the season of the Lakota hunt she taught.

Peacemaker (Haudenosaunee) - Markers you carry: symbolic only. You were born in western Pennsylvania, in lands the Seneca once claimed. - Markers you carry, by story: the Peacemaker was born to a virgin mother, and your mother is named Mary; his message joined five nations under one law, and your Sun is in Libra, the sign of law and balance.

Rua Kēnana, as Te Kooti's successor - Markers you carry: born in the East (the eastern United States), the direction Te Kooti named for his successor.

Return of Lono (Hawaiʻi) - Markers you carry: Makahiki timing. The season opens when the Pleiades rise at dusk (about 15 November in 1998); you were born on 13 October, five weeks before it opens, in the month that prepares the Makahiki.

"Rainbow Warriors" - Markers you carry: none of the specific markers. - Status of the source: this comes from Willoya and Brown's 1962 tract and is documented as fakelore (Niman). It is not an authentic Native prophecy.

Final integrated reading

Across eleven traditions, the same picture keeps coming back. You were born:

  • at the waning, gathering end of a lunar month (ʻOlekūlua / Korekore-hahani)
  • at the harvest turn of the year (Kenténha, the Lenape ceremony, the Cherokee Harvest Moon, Čhaŋwápekasná Wí)
  • before the Pleiades returned (before Makahiki, Matariʻi i raro)
  • under Hōkūleʻa, the star that brings voyagers home

None of these traditions assigns a fate at birth. What they offer is a seasonal identity: someone who brings things in, finishes the work, and finds the way home. The only prophecy markers you carry personally are being male and coming from the East in the Pahana account, alongside your generational share of the Seventh Fire.
