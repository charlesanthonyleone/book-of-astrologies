1. The Jain sky: what kind of astrology this is

Jain jyotiṣa does not begin with a horoscope. It begins with a cosmos. The early sources (SP, CP, JP) are astronomical canons. They are meant to explain the movement of the luminaries for monastic timekeeping: when to fast, when to recite, when the rainy-season retreat begins. They do not predict personal destiny. Predictive astrology arrives later, in the Bhadrabāhu Saṃhitā (omens), Udayaprabha (election of times) and Hemaprabha (annual Tājika charts). Underneath all of it lies karma. In Jain doctrine the stars do not cause anything; they indicate the ripening of karma the soul already carries. Every "what this means for you" below should be read that way: these are signs of a ripening, not sentences passed on you.

What this means for you. Interpretation In this school, your chart is a weather report on your karma. It is not a fate. Jain teaching insists that effort (puruṣārtha), vows and restraint change the outcome. So the sharpest readings in this chapter are invitations to act, not verdicts.

2. Jambūdvīpa and the two suns at your birth

Source JP 7 and SP 1 say that Jambūdvīpa, the central round continent 100,000 yojanas across with Mount Meru at its centre, is lit by two suns and two moons. They sit on opposite sides of Meru: one lights Bharata (our southern region) while the other lights Airāvata in the north, and they trade places every 30 muhūrtas.

Calculated On 13 Oct 1998 the Sun had travelled 114.3 days since the summer solstice, which puts it on maṇḍala 115 of 184. The innermost circle is 99,640 yojanas across and each circle adds 5 35/61, so the Sun's orbit diameter was 100,275 yojanas and its speed 5,285 yojanas per muhūrta. The SP says a person in Bharata can see the Sun from 37,688 yojanas away (cakṣuḥsparśa).

Finding: which sun is ours. Source + Convention adopted The SP does not name the two suns as fixed individuals with a running count; it defines them by function: whichever sun is over Bharata is the Bharata-sun (bhāratīya sūrya), the other is the Airāvata-sun. Johnstown and Fayetteville lie in our Bharata-side hemisphere of the Jain map, and your birth fell in daylight (17:25 EDT). So by the text's own definition the sun lighting your birth is the Bharata-sun, and that is "ours." No text supplies a day count that could tell the two suns apart as individuals, so this chapter treats the question as settled by definition.

What this means for you. Interpretation You were born when the suns were well outside the middle of their track, heading toward the outer, winter circles. The Jain astronomers read that as shortening light and a lengthening night. In this school's idiom it marks a soul that comes into its own through inward work, not public brightness. Your best light is reflected light: your gifts show most clearly in how they illuminate others.

Interpretation An "empty" tithi is classically avoided for starting ventures. Its own day-name, though, is Dhanañjaya, "winner of wealth", and its night-twin is Sarvasiddhā: the Jain calendar itself pairs the empty vessel with the winning. For a birth, Jain commentators read it as a soul that has to fill its own vessel. Little comes ready-made; everything is earned. Sādhya ("accomplishable") yoga balances it: what you set out to do can be done.

Finding: the yuga year. Source + Convention adopted The Jain 5-year yuga runs Candra, Candra, Abhivardhita, Candra, Abhivardhita, and SP says a yuga begins on Śrāvaṇa kṛṣṇa 1 with the Moon in Abhijit. No unbroken yuga count survives, so this chapter anchors the cycle at Vīra Nirvāṇa (VNS year 1 = yuga year 1), the one epoch every Jain calendar shares. On that anchor VNS 2524 is yuga year 4: Candra, an ordinary lunar year with no intercalary month, which agrees with the computed fact that 1998–99 had no adhika month at your birth. On the 60-year cycle the year is Bahudhānya ("abundant grain").

Calculated Vīra Nirvāṇa Saṃvat 2524 (VNS = CE + 527 after Dīvālī; your birth fell before Dīvālī 1998, so CE 1998 + 526 → 2524 counting the nirvāṇa year as year 1 in the common Jain calendar).

What this means for you. Interpretation An empty tithi inside an abundant year: plenty is around you, and the work is learning to gather it. A Tuesday birth puts Mars, the planet of effort, in charge of the day, which fits the Riktā message.

Synthesis (§§5–6). The time-units all agree on three things: custodianship (Vaiśramaṇa), self-cultivation (Bhāvitātmā), and effort that fills what starts out empty (Riktā tithi, Tuesday). That is the Jain ideal of puruṣārtha, written into the clock.

7. The kalpa wheel: where you stand in cosmic time

Source Time turns as a wheel. The descending half-cycle (avasarpiṇī) and the ascending half-cycle (utsarpiṇī) each have six aras, from suṣamā-suṣamā (bliss-bliss) down to duḥṣamā-duḥṣamā (misery-misery). We are in the 5th ara, Duḥṣamā, which began 3 years 8½ months after Mahāvīra's nirvāṇa (527 BCE) and lasts 21,000 years.

Calculated You were born 2,521 years into Duḥṣamā, with 18,479 years of it left. - The 6th ara begins about 20,478 CE. - The utsarpiṇī begins about 41,478 CE. - Its 3rd ara, when Padmanābha is born, begins about 83,478 CE.

What this means for you. Interpretation In Jain doctrine no one born in Bharata during the 5th ara reaches liberation in that same life. The path is still open, though. You can practise right faith, knowledge and conduct and so shorten the soul's journey. Your era is a hard school, which is exactly why effort counts for so much in it.
