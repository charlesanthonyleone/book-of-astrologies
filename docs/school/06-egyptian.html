2. The 36 decans

2.1 Diagonal star clocks, Book of Nut, Seti I, Tanis and Ramesside lists Source, verify

The decans (bꜣkw, 'workers', or sbꜣw) were 36 star groups rising in turn for ten days each. They are tabled on Middle Kingdom coffin lids as diagonal star clocks (Asyut, c. 2100 BC). The Book of Nut (Cenotaph of Seti I, Abydos; tomb of Ramesses IV) describes their 70 days of invisibility as a stay in the Duat, purification and rebirth. The Seti I family (Seti I, Ramesses II and later ceilings), the Tanis family (Tanis papyrus, Greek-period monuments) and the Ramesside star clocks (tombs of Ramesses VI, VII and IX, with stars at 'the right shoulder', 'the heart' and so on) each preserve variant lists. Under the Ptolemies the decans were attached to the zodiac at Dendera, and the Hellenistic 10° decans descend from them. Iconography Source, verify: decans are standing or seated deities, often snake-headed, lion-headed or ram-headed, sailing in barques. Sothis/Sopdet is a woman with a tall plume or star on a cow in a boat, Sah (Orion) a man striding and looking back, and Kenmut a lion or baboon form.

7. Birth fate — Shai, Renenutet, Meskhenet and the seven Hathors Source, verify

Shai (šꜣy, 'what is decreed') is the god of a person's allotted fate. Renenutet, the cobra harvest-goddess, nurses and names the child and gives its fortune (rnnt). Meskhenet, the birth-brick, decides destiny at the birth (Westcar). The seven Hathors announce the child's fate at the cradle (Tale of the Doomed Prince; Tale of Two Brothers). In Graeco-Egyptian texts Shai is equated with the Agathos Daimon and Renenutet with Tyche. Astrologically that means the 11th place (Good Daimon) and the Lot of Fortune.

15. Integrated Reading Interpretation

Read in Egyptian order (placement, decans, calendars, fate, gods, prophecy), the chart tells one story. You are born as Horus rises. The light over the Two Lands, Jupiter, stands a degree under the eastern horizon in his own watery sign, in the last decan of the year, the one that closes the circle before Sopdet returns. Your fate-gods, Shai and Renenutet, sit together in the Good Daimon's place. Both your birth days are wholly good in the Cairo Calendar. The dream book makes you a Horus man, and the prophetic literature recognises you in the rising child of Chemmis and in the Potter's Oracle king who comes from the Sun.

The rest of the chart is a Duat pattern. Ra and the Morning god work in the late afternoon, in the Balance, out of public view: they are strong in shadow. Red Horus has gone into the west, and the Moon is a late, waning crescent in Khnum's decan. Your force, your moods and your deepest gifts mature in hidden places and come out at the turn of a cycle (70 days after Sothis, decan-week 7, day-hour 11, lunar day 24). The heavy Bull of the Sky is tamed.

So the life pattern is renewal. You do best when completing one cycle and opening the next, as a restorer of balance (Maat), a protector (Chnoumis), and someone whose open face and generosity open doors before he speaks. The Horus myth's defeat of Seth comes to you as it came to Horus, by judgement in the Scales. Patience and appearing at the right time are your native weapons.
