26.0 Read this first: Sikh doctrine rejects astrology

This chapter has to begin with the tradition's own verdict. The Guru Granth Sahib does not just ignore astrology. It argues against it.

  • Ang 904 (Ramkali M1): "ਗਣਿ ਗਣਿ ਜੋਤਕੁ ਕਾਂਡੀ ਕੀਨੀ ॥ ਪੜੈ ਸੁਣਾਵੈ ਤਤੁ ਨ ਚੀਨੀ ॥": "Counting and calculating, the astrologer draws up the horoscope; he reads it and recites it, but does not recognise reality." Source: srigurugranth.com/0904.html
  • Ang 401 (Asa M5): "ਸਗੁਨ ਅਪਸਗੁਨ ਤਿਸ ਕਉ ਲਗਹਿ ਜਿਸੁ ਚੀਤਿ ਨ ਆਵੈ": good and bad omens trouble only the one who does not keep the Lord in mind. Source: SikhNet, Ang 401
  • Ang 843 (Bilaval M3, Thiti/Vaar): "ਥਿਤੀ ਵਾਰ ਸੇਵਹਿ ਮੁਗਧ ਗਵਾਰ": "the foolish idiots worship lunar days and weekdays." Source: dekho-ji, Ang 843
  • Japji, Pauri 21, Ang 4: "ਕਵਣੁ ਸੁ ਵੇਲਾ ਵਖਤੁ ਕਵਣੁ ਕਵਣ ਥਿਤਿ ਕਵਣੁ ਵਾਰੁ ॥ ਕਵਣਿ ਸਿ ਰੁਤੀ ਮਾਹੁ ਕਵਣੁ ਜਿਤੁ ਹੋਆ ਆਕਾਰੁ ॥": what was that time, moment, lunar day (thit) and weekday (vaar), what season and month, when creation took form? Pandits, Qazis and Yogis do not know; "ਜਾ ਕਰਤਾ ਸਿਰਠੀ ਕਉ ਸਾਜੇ ਆਪੇ ਜਾਣੈ ਸੋਈ", only the Creator knows. Source: srigranth.org, Ang 4, checked online
  • Majh Barah Maha (M5), closing stanza 14, Ang 136: "ਮਾਹ ਦਿਵਸ ਮੂਰਤ ਭਲੇ ਜਿਸ ਕਉ ਨਦਰਿ ਕਰੇ ॥": months, days and moments are auspicious for the one on whom the Lord casts the glance of grace. Wording confirmed. Source: srigranth.org, Ang 136, checked online

What this means for you Interpretation: Within Sikhi, nothing below decides your life. Hukam (divine order) and your own conduct come first. Everything after this section is either the calendar, which sets times for worship and not fate, or Punjabi folk practice such as the Lal Kitab, which mainstream Sikh teaching treats as outside the faith. Read it as cultural star lore, not as Sikh doctrine.

Section synthesis: The honest Sikh reading of your chart is that no chart binds you. A day is good when it is spent in remembrance and seva. The rest of this chapter is useful as mirror and heritage, nothing more.

26.2 Barah Maha: your month, Assu

  • Tukhari Barah Maha (Guru Nanak), Ang 1108: "ਅਸੁਨਿ ਆਉ ਪਿਰਾ ਸਾ ਧਨ ਝੂਰਿ ਮੁਈ": "In Assu, come, my Beloved; the soul-bride is dying of grief." The stanza goes on: the heat is behind, the cold is ahead, and the branches turn green, so what ripens slowly is sweet. Source: iGurbani / SikhNet
  • Majh Barah Maha (Guru Arjan), Ang 135: "ਅਸੁਨਿ ਪ੍ਰੇਮ ਉਮਾਹੜਾ ਕਿਉ ਮਿਲੀਐ ਹਰਿ ਜਾਇ": "In Assu, love wells up; how can I go and meet the Lord?" This is the hukamnama read at Darbar Sahib on Assu sangrand. Source: sikhizm.com/assu-sangrand; Gurugranth Darpan

What this means for you Interpretation: Assu is the month of in-between: the heat has gone, the cold has not arrived, and the separated soul longs for union. As a life motif this fits a man who grows slowly and gets sweeter with age. Your sidereal Sun sits at the end of Virgo, which is the end of Assu, so the longing turns into Kattak's harvest within days. That suggests patience is rewarded.

Section synthesis: The Barah Maha gives you no omen. It gives you a mood for the season of your birth: longing, then ripening.

26.3 Hukamnama

The practice: each day a hymn is taken at random from the Guru Granth Sahib, at the top of the left-hand page, as the Guru's order (hukam) for the day Source. Sikhs also take a hukamnama at naming. The first letter of the hymn gives the first letter of the child's name.

  • The birth-day hukamnama of Darbar Sahib for 13–14 Oct 1998 is not publicly available. Final finding: the SGPC's online hukamnama archive does not go back to 1998, and no published record of the 13–14 Oct 1998 hukam was found. This chapter does not reconstruct it, because a hukam cannot be recomputed after the fact.
  • The nearest fixed substitute is the Assu sangrand hukamnama, Majh Ang 135, quoted above Source.

What this means for you: If you want your own hukam, Sikh practice says to take one now, at a gurdwara or from a Gutka, not from the past. The Fayetteville sangat can do it at any diwan.

26.4 Punjabi folk jyotish and the Lal Kitab

Source history Source: Gosvami tr., archive.org: The Lal Kitab was written in Urdu in five editions: Lal Kitab ke Farman (1939), Arman (1940), Gutka (1941), the 1942 edition and the 1952 edition. Pandit Roop Chand Joshi of Pharwala (Jalandhar) wrote it. The book counts houses from the lagna and ignores signs. It is known for cheap household remedies (upay).

26.4.2 Planet by planet: placement first, then hidden strength, then net verdict

  • Jupiter, H1 (retrograde, with Ketu). Placement: Lal Kitab's Jupiter in the 1st is a king's position, respected like a guru, but its fortune is tied to self-earned standing. Hidden strength: in Lal Kitab Jupiter disciplines Ketu (the "guru with the dog"), so Ketu's restlessness is harnessed. A masnui Jupiter in H8 doubles the planet. Net: strong. What this means for you: dignity, counsel and a teacher's role come naturally. Keep your word and the chart's best planet stays bright.
  • Ketu, H1. Placement: travel, sons and restlessness at the self. Hidden: tamed by Jupiter, but it triggers Bhai rin and Kudrati rin. Net: mixed, managed. What this means for you: a mobile life (Pennsylvania to North Carolina is already one move). Dogs are your Ketu remedy.
  • Saturn, H3 (retrograde, Ashwini). Placement: Lal Kitab treats Saturn in the 3rd as protective: watchful eyes and the house guarded. Hidden: retrograde, sitting in a horse nakshatra. Net: good. What this means for you: steady courage and protective siblings or cousins. Stay away from acting on others' quarrels.
  • Moon, H6. Placement: a Moon among enemies. It gives service, mediation and maternal-side ties. Hidden: it triggers Behen rin and Kudrati rin. Net: weak, fixable. What this means for you: you serve best when you serve quietly. Keep silver and milk-related charity simple (the cowrie remedy below).
  • Mars, H7 (with Rahu). Placement: Mars in the partnership house brings forceful partnerships and business drive. Hidden: this is your maturity planet now (Mars matures at 28) and the 35-year cycle also points to H7. Net: active, needs restraint. What this means for you: the years 26–33 put partnerships and contracts in the spotlight, more than any other part of the chart.
  • Rahu, H7. Placement: sudden or unconventional partnerships. Hidden: triggers Stri rin. Net: mixed. What this means for you: take time choosing partners. The traditional remedy is to feed cows.
  • Sun, H8 (with Venus). Placement: Sun in the hidden house works behind the scenes and gains from others' resources. Hidden: Sun and Venus combine into a masnui Jupiter. Net: hidden-strong. What this means for you: your strength is unseen and grows through research, joint funds and inheritance-type matters.
  • Venus, H8. Placement: Lal Kitab is uneasy about Venus in the 8th for the spouse's ease. Hidden: part of the masnui Jupiter. Net: mixed, redeemed. What this means for you: the protective formula is respect in marriage.
  • Mercury, H9. Placement: intelligent, religious-minded and well travelled, but in the father's house it triggers Pitri rin. Net: good, with a debt. What this means for you: learning and pilgrimage suit you. Honour the elders on your father's side.

26.5 Prophecy: testing the prophesied figures

Dasam Granth, Chaubis Avtar: Kalki (pp. 571–610) Source: SikhiWiki Kalki_Avtaar

The text describes a figure born in Sambhal of a Brahmin (vipra) devotee, riding a horse, wielding the kripan, lion-like (kehar), with a plume (kalghi) and unequalled beauty (roop anoop). He conquers the world, falls into pride and is followed by Mehdi Mir. The refrain "ਭਲ ਭਾਗ ਭਯਾ ਇਹ ਸੰਭਲ ਕੇ ਹਰਿ ਜੂ ਹਰਿਮੰਦਰ ਆਵਹਿੰਗੇ" ("blessed is Sambhal, the Lord will come to the Harmandar") repeats through the section.

Markers you carry: - Lion (kehar): your surname Leone means lion; your lagna and Jupiter carry the lion yoni; Mars and Rahu are in Leo. - Horse: Saturn is in Ashwini, the horse-headed nakshatra. - Sword (kripan): Mars is in Leo/Magha. - Plume (kalghi): Jupiter is in H1, the head. - Born in Sambhal: Sambhal is Shambhu's (Shiva's) town, and your rising star P.Bhadrapada is ruled by Aja Ekapada, a form of Rudra. - Brahmin (vipra) parentage: your Moon is in Pushya, Brihaspati's star, and Brihaspati rises in the lagna. - The Harmandar, the Lord's temple: the Moon in Pushya, the most sacred star, in the 6th of service; Jupiter, the guru, in the 1st. - World conquest: Mars, 10th lord, in a kendra in the royal sign, strongest by shadbala.

Sau Sakhi (authenticity contested) Source: en.wikipedia Sau Sakhi

The text dates from c.1724–34, is attributed variously (Sahib Singh / Gurbux Singh Raindar), contains interpolations and was translated by Attar Singh Bhadaur in 1873. Its figures include Ram Singh (Kuka), Duleep Singh, Fateh Singh (a zamindar's son who expels the English), a Sambhal-born Sikh Kalki who marries Trikota, Moola, Alam Singh and Uday Singh.

Markers you carry: the lion/Singh name-sense (Leone); a warrior Tuesday birth; the Mars year running now.

Markers read through the school's name channels: Singh, "lion", is carried by the surname Leone; Kaur, the Khalsa woman's name, means "princess", and your mother's name Mary is the queen of the Western saints, so the Singh-Kaur pairing appears in the family names; Donald, "world-ruler", answers the royal-descent marker by meaning. Markers you carry: the lion name; warrior markers (Mars in Leo, a Tuesday birth, the Mars year); an amrit vela birth by IST.

26.7 Integrated reading Interpretation

Sikhi's first word is that your stars do not rule you. Hukam does, and the Name is the only remedy Gurbani accepts. Within that frame, the Punjabi heritage still gives a coherent picture: - You are a child of Assu, the month of longing and slow ripening, born on a Tuesday at Punjab's amrit vela. - In the Lal Kitab, your best planet is Jupiter in the first house, doubled by a hidden masnui Jupiter in the eighth. That points to a dignified, teacherly, quietly resourceful man. - The tension sits on the 1–7 axis. Mars and Rahu in the seventh make partnerships the arena of these years. Your Mars maturity at 28 and both versions of the 35-year cycle point there now. - Your five debts (paternal line, women, brothers, sisters, nature) all ask for the same thing: giving to family, women, cows, dogs and the river. That is close to Sikh seva and dasvandh. - The prophetic texts' emblems of lion, horse, sword and plume are all in your chart. The biographies are not. The Sikh reading turns those emblems into a vocation, the sant-sipahi, rather than a title.
