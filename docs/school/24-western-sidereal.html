1. The school in brief

Source Cyril Fagan (1896–1970), an Irish astrologer, revived the sidereal zodiac in the West from 1944. His case was that the zodiac of Egypt and Babylon was fixed to the stars, with Spica at 29° Virgo and Aldebaran/Antares at 15° Taurus/Scorpio. He set this out in Zodiacs Old and New (1951), drawing on the Dendera zodiac and on Babylonian texts. Donald A. Bradley (1925–1974), who wrote as Garth Allen, joined him. Together they fixed the Fagan/Allen ayanamsa, with SVP 335°57'28.64" at 1950.0. Bradley wrote Solar and Lunar Returns (1948) and supplied the school's predictive tools: precession-corrected (PC) returns, Campanus houses, foreground angularity, solunars, kinetic returns and quotidians. Brigadier R. C. Firebrace co-wrote the Primer of Sidereal Astrology with Fagan and edited the journal Spica from 1961 to 1974. Kenneth Bowser carried the method forward in An Introduction to Western Sidereal Astrology (2012) and in the Q2 work at solunars.com. Jim Eshelman and others extended the solunar system.

What this means for you: in this school the star background, not the seasons, decides your signs. Almost every planet of yours moves back one sign, and the angles become the main source of strength.

3. Natal chart and Bradley angularity

Source Bradley's rules: strength comes from closeness to an angle in mundane position: foreground within about 10°, extended to 15°. By the house version, angular houses are foreground, succedent houses middleground and cadent houses background. Bradley ignored house rulerships. He counted Venus, Jupiter and Uranus as benefics and Mars, Saturn and Neptune as malefics, and used only the five major aspects (astrologysoftware.com, Precessed Return Charts; Fagan & Firebrace, Primer).

Mars at the western angle brings competitive energy into partnerships and open dealings with others.
